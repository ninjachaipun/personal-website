Underwriting Judgment

Key Considerations & Conclusion

On paper, this is the strongest-returning deal I've modeled so far — but it's also the one with the least verified information behind it. The brochure leans almost entirely on a 67% rental upside claim, and I have very little independent data to check that number against.

  • No occupancy or lease detail disclosed: the brochure states a $517,091 Year 1 NOI with no occupancy rate, no lease terms, and no rent roll. I can't verify that NOI, or the timeline to realize any upside, without knowing who's actually in the building today.
  • The 67% rental upside is the seller's number, not mine: it drives most of the growth in this model — NOI nearly doubles between year 2 and year 3 on the assumption that rents catch up to market by then. The brochure cites “prevailing market rental rates” without disclosing current rents or comparables, and Ontario's rent guideline limits on existing tenancies make the actual timing uncertain.
  • Property tax is an unverified placeholder: unlike my Dundas model, where I could at least back into an assessed value from a public tax bill, this brochure discloses no taxes and no assessed value at all. The $116,951/yr figure here is a multi-residential-rate estimate on an assumed assessment, nothing more.
  • Unit sizes and GLA aren't usable: the brochure gives unit counts by type but no square footage, and the source spreadsheet's own GLA figure implies roughly 57 sq. ft. per unit — clearly wrong, so I've left it off the Property Overview above.
  • Major systems condition isn't disclosed: recent capital work (elevator, roof, some electrical panels) is listed, but the brochure says nothing about the remaining life of boilers, risers, or windows on a 67-year-old building. No capital reserve study is mentioned.
  • Development upside on the rear lot is explicitly unentitled: the brochure itself notes any intensification is “subject to the purchaser's independent planning and zoning review” with no development entitlement to be assumed — so I've treated this as a bonus, not part of the base case.

My Take

This is the best-looking return of the three deals I've modeled, and the leverage here is working the way it's supposed to — the levered IRR is well above the unlevered IRR, which wasn't true for Dundas. But almost every input that makes this deal look good — the 67% upside, the $517K NOI, the tax bill — comes from the seller's own claims with nothing independent behind it yet. If I were actually pursuing this, the very first call would be to the listing agent for a real rent roll and the last three years of financials; until then, I'd treat this model as a best-case scenario, not a base case.
